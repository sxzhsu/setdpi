300 DPI won't fix low-res art

If a customer sends a 600 px logo, setting 300 DPI just tells the RIP it's 2 inches wide. For a full front print (about 12 in), you need around 3600 px across. Get a bigger file or vectorize it — then set DPI here.

Placement Typical print width Pixels across at 300 DPI

Hat front 4–4.5 in 1200–1350 px

Gang sheet planning note: A 22 × 60 inch transfer film roll requires a 6600 × 18000 pixel canvas at 300 DPI. If your individual graphic elements are tagged at 72 DPI, RIP software will place them oversized on the film until corrected.

DTF Printing FAQ

Will my PNG keep its transparent background?
Yes. SetDPI only rewrites the PNG's density field (the pHYs chunk). The image data — including the alpha channel — is copied byte for byte. Transparent stays transparent, soft edges stay soft.
Should I convert my DTF file to CMYK?
No. PNG doesn't support CMYK, and DTF RIP software expects RGB PNGs — it handles the CMYK + white ink separation itself. Keep your files in sRGB and let the RIP do its job.
What do I need for a 22-inch gang sheet?
At 300 DPI, a full 22-inch-wide sheet is 6600 pixels across. A 22 × 60 in sheet works out to 6600 × 18000 px. If you build gang sheets in Photoshop or Illustrator, set the document to 300 PPI from the start — then use SetDPI to correct any single designs that come in tagged 72.
My customer's file is 72 DPI. Is it ruined?
Not necessarily. Check the pixel width first. If it's 3600 px wide, it's a perfectly good 12-inch print — it just has the wrong tag. Set it to 300 here. If it's 800 px wide, the tag isn't the problem: ask for a bigger file or a vector.
